# Cataloging three years of Elering coverage under Kalle Kilk

**Kalle Kilk has led Elering AS since 1 July 2023 — not April 2024 as originally assumed — so the press catalog below spans April 2023 (succession announcement, 25 April 2023) through September 2026.** Kilk, an Elering management-board member since 13 February 2007, succeeded Taavi Veskimägi as chairman of the management board (CEO) and was re-elected after a public competition on **11 October 2024 with a five-year contract through 31 December 2029** ([Elering](https://elering.ee/en/article/supervisory-board-elering-re-elected-kalle-kilk-chairman-board)). Coverage across English, Estonian and Russian outlets clusters around six storylines: the **BRELL desynchronization and synchronization with Continental Europe on 8–9 February 2025** (Kilk: "the hardest thing we've done this century"); the **Estlink 2 cable cut by the tanker Eagle S on 25 December 2024** and its ahead-of-schedule €50–60m repair (June 2025); the **Balticconnector gas pipeline's return to service on 22 April 2024** after the October 2023 anchor damage; the **37.6% transmission-tariff rise** (applied for July 2025, approved 1 February 2026, effective 5 May 2026) plus two new balancing fees from 2026; the **~900 MW reserve/gas power plant and frequency-reserve program** (~€1bn); and the **slipping Estlink 3 / fourth Estonia–Latvia interconnector timelines** (investment decisions ~2031–2033, completion possibly 2038–2040). ERR News is the systematic English record of Kilk's statements; Estonian coverage is dominated by ERR and Postimees; Russian-language coverage splits sharply between Baltic outlets (where Kilk is front-and-center) and RF state media (where he is nearly invisible and Elering's own cost disclosures are used as anti-Baltic ammunition). The full catalog — roughly 120 distinct items, every URL taken directly from the research notes, none invented — follows the briefing.

## Briefing: the corrected leadership timeline and boards

The task's original premise that Kilk became CEO around April 2024 conflates his 2024 re-election competition with his actual start. Veskimägi announced on **25 April 2023** that he would step down after ~14 years, effective 1 July 2023 ([ERR News](https://news.err.ee/1608958723/elering-ceo-taavi-veskimagi-to-step-down-after-14-years); [rus.err.ee](https://rus.err.ee/1608958678/v-ijule-taavi-veskimjagi-pokinet-dolzhnost-predsedatelja-pravlenija-elering)). From **1 July 2023** the board continued with **Kilk as chair** (initially to end-2024), Riina Käi (CFO) and new member Erkki Sapp; Veskimägi stayed on as adviser to the synchronization project ([Elering, 29 June 2023](https://elering.ee/en/article/board-elering-will-continue-renewed-composition-july-1)). After a public competition (candidate evaluation by Fontes PMP), the supervisory board **re-elected Kilk by consensus on 11 October 2024, through 31 December 2029** ([Elering](https://elering.ee/en/article/supervisory-board-elering-re-elected-kalle-kilk-chairman-board); [ERR](https://www.err.ee/1609487971/eleringi-juhina-jatkab-kalle-kilk)).

The **management board (juhatus)** is four-strong: Kilk (chair, to 31.12.2029); **Riina Käi** (finance, board member since 03.01.2020, term to 31.12.2026); **Erkki Sapp** (energy system management and markets, since 04.07.2023, term to 31.12.2026); and **Reigo Kebja** (asset management — the portfolio Kilk himself previously held — appointed 05.02.2024, term to 04.02.2029) ([Elering, 11 Oct 2024](https://elering.ee/en/article/supervisory-board-elering-re-elected-kalle-kilk-chairman-board); [Inforegister](https://www.inforegister.ee/en/11022625-ELERING-AS/)). Kebja's February 2024 appointment is the only board addition in the window; no press release announcing it was located, and no coverage was found on whether Käi's and Sapp's mandates (expiring 31 December 2026) will be renewed — a decision due just after the research window.

The **supervisory board (nõukogu)** is chaired by **Gunnar Okk** (registry entry 04.03.2024; ERR reported his becoming chair with a term given as 9 Feb 2024 – 8 Feb 2027 per the Finance Ministry list, while a fin.ee state-assets table shows a mandate 14.10.2024–13.10.2027 — the discrepancy is unreconciled, possibly initial appointment then renewal) ([ERR](https://www.err.ee/1609248744/gunnar-okk-saab-eleringi-noukogu-esimeheks); [Inforegister](https://www.inforegister.ee/en/11022625-ELERING-AS/); [fin.ee PDF](https://fin.ee/sites/default/files/documents/2025-10/Ariyhingute_noukogud_veebi_sept2025.pdf)). Members joined in waves: Sirli Männiksaar (04.03.2024), Jaanus Uiga (09.10.2024), Merike Saks (14.03.2025), Veiko Haavapuu (15.05.2026) — four of five seats turned over in 2024–2026, but no press coverage naming the departing members was found. Chair remuneration is €1,500/month, members €750/month (figure partly from search snippet; verify against the PDF).

**Financials under Kilk** (from Elering's investor page, extracted by automated summary — spot-check against annual reports before publication): 2024 revenue €214.3m, EBITDA €70.8m, net profit €15.7m, investments €240.8m, congestion income €80.8m, dividends paid €20.0m; 2025 revenue €300.5m, EBITDA €80.5m, net profit €21.7m, investments €181.0m, congestion income €6.7m, dividends paid €15.7m ([Elering, For investors](https://elering.ee/en/investors)). The **collapse of congestion income (€80.8m → €6.7m)** is the structural driver of the 2026 tariff hike. Moody's rates Elering A2/stable (affirmation reported Feb 2024). Estonia's share of the ~€1.6bn Baltic synchronization program was **€298m, ~75% EU-funded** ([Kliimaministeerium](https://kliimaministeerium.ee/uudised/oleme-valmis-baltimaade-sunkroniseerimiseks-euroopa-elektrivorguga); [European Commission Estonia](https://estonia.representation.ec.europa.eu/uudised/balti-riigid-uhinesid-mandri-euroopa-elektrivorguga-2025-02-09_et); [Konkurentsiamet yearbook](https://aastaraamat.konkurentsiamet.ee/en/aastaraamat-2024/synchronisation-baltic-states-electricity-system-continental-europe)).

**Date and sourcing conventions used throughout the catalog:** dates marked "approx." or "~" are inferred from article content, IDs or adjacent events, not fetched pages; every URL was actually seen in search results or fetches by the researchers — none are invented; items recorded from search metadata only (paywalled or blocked pages) are flagged as such.

## Gaps, flags and verification notes

## Conclusion

Three years of coverage show Kilk converting a technical infrastructure job into one of Estonia's most visible public roles: the same executive who warned in autumn 2023 that leaving the Russian grid "could leave our rooms dark" delivered the February 2025 switchover so cleanly that his emotional Narva interview ran simultaneously in Estonian and Russian Postimees — and Elering's deliberate messaging to the Russian-speaking minority (a bylined Kilk op-ed on rus.err.ee) was a distinct communications operation, not incidental translation. The catalog also exposes the story's second act: Kilk's pre-switchover promise that desynchronization "will not raise tariffs" now coexists with the 37.6% transmission-tariff rise, two new balancing fees, and a ~€1bn reserve-plant bill — precisely the tension RF state media mine, using Elering's own disclosures while ignoring Kilk himself.

For anyone extending this research, the leverage points are clear: the paywalled Estonian and Russian-language Delfi ecosystems are the largest verified hole; the late-2026 board-renewal decision (Käi and Sapp, terms expiring 31 December 2026) is the next governance event the catalog cannot yet answer; and the Eagle S trial verdict and the first full year under the new tariff regime (from 5 May 2026) are the two storylines most likely to generate the next wave of cross-language coverage.
